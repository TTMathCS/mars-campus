Chapter 03

The Pentagon

Arcadia below ground. One solid five-sided block, 160 m on each side and five levels deep, lies under the Stone Garden with 16 m of soil on top. It is Arcadia by night and at work: where Jim sleeps, where the gardens and the workshops are, and where the air, water and power come from. It has eleven times the floor area of the Crown.

The planFive rings, five sectors, one atrium

Every level has the same plan. Round the atrium in the middle run five pentagonal rings, A on the inside to E on the outside, each 14 m deep, with 4 m streets between them. Five 5 m avenues run from the atrium's corners out to the five corner cores, and divide each level into five sectors.

Each corner core sits under one of the Crown's spires and holds a portal to that spire, a portal to every other level, a stair, and the shafts for air, water and power. In the middle of the atrium stands the portal column, with a portal on every level reached by five bridges.

Sector 1 lies to the south-east, under the Crown's Master suite up, and the sectors are numbered clockwise from there. Jim's own rooms on L1 are in sector 1, straight below his suite in the Crown.

Why undergroundSixteen metres of soil

Radiation. On the open plain cosmic rays and solar storms give about 230 mSv a year. Under 16 m of soil, about 2,700 g/cm², that falls to less than people get on Earth. Nothing else Jim could build stops radiation so completely.

Pressure. Air inside pushes the roof up; soil on top pushes it down. At Earth's sea-level pressure the two balance under 16 m of soil:

101 kPa ÷ (1.7 t/m³ × 3.71 m/s²) ≈ 16 m

At 70 kPa, the pressure chosen for Arcadia (chapter 08), 11 m would balance and the extra 5 m simply rests on the roof. Either way the roof never has to hold the Pentagon down against its own air, which is the hardest job a Mars building has.

Temperature. A few metres down the day and the seasons disappear; the ground stays near −60 °C all year. The Pentagon's walls are insulated so its warmth doesn't melt the ice-rich ground round it, just as buildings on permafrost in Canada and Siberia are.

Storms and quakes. Dust storms don't reach it, and the small marsquakes at this distance are felt less below ground than on the surface.

The heartThe atrium and the sun court

The atrium is a five-sided shaft 35 m across and 68 m deep. Light from the Sun Well's sky lens falls down its middle; the portal column rises through it; five bridges cross to the column on every level. Its walls are terraces of hanging gardens that catch the light, with water running down them from the lake on L2.

At the bottom, on L5, the sun court is a garden of 2,100 m² under the sky lens: lawns, fruit trees and a pool under the column, lit with the colour and warmth of a clear Mars noon.

The lens shows the sky that is outside, sunrise to sunset; in a dust storm it keeps the same bright sky for as long as the storm lasts. Jim takes the real sun up in the Crown and the Orb.

A portal is a door that opens onto another place: step through the one in the corner core and you are in the spire above, or on another level. Portals link the Crown's spires, the Orb, the corner cores and the portal column. You walk through them in one step.

Real fallback

Every corner core also has a stair from L5 to L1, and robot carriers climb the spiral ramps beside it with goods. From L1, a sealed stair in each core comes up through an airlock into the corner pavilion on the surface. If the portals stop, you can still walk anywhere below ground, and the pods and escape capsules connect the Crown to the ground (chapter 02).

Safety. Each sector can be sealed from the others by pressure doors in the streets and avenues, so a leak or a fire stays in one sector. Every point has two ways out. The storm reserve on L4 holds food and spares for two years.

How it is builtDig, build, cover

The Pentagon is built in an open pit by robots, then buried.

  1. Dig. Excavators and cutters remove about 3 million m³ of ground to 70 m. In the frozen ice-rich ground the pit walls stand steep on their own, like an ice quarry.
  2. Keep the ice. Most of what comes out below 14 m is water ice, about 1.5 million tonnes: water, air and rocket fuel for decades. It goes to the ice store and the fuel plant.
  3. Build. The walls, floors and roof are thick sintered-regolith panels made in the foundry from the soil that was dug out, tied together as one stiff block, then lined, insulated and sealed.
  4. Cover. The roof is covered with 16 m of the dug soil, rolled in layers. On top go a paved pentagon 4 m wider than the block, so the ground shows where Arcadia lies, the Stone Garden inside it, the Orb's dock round the Sun Well, a glass pavilion over each corner stair, and the Crown's pads.

Digging starts in 2024, a year after the first robots land and three before Jim arrives (chapter 10). L4 and L5, which hold life support and the transit halls, are finished first; the gardens take the longest to grow.
