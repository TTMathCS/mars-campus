Talking to Earth

Minutes away, by radio and laser. No phone calls: messages, video letters and email.

The delay3 to 22 minutes each way

Radio travels at the speed of light, and Mars is 55 to 401 million km away, so a signal takes 3 to 22 minutes each way, depending on where the planets are. A question and its answer take 6 to 44 minutes, so there are no live conversations.

Behind the Sun. Every 26 months Mars passes behind the Sun as seen from Earth, and the Sun's glare drowns the signal. Contact drops for about two weeks; missions plan quiet work for that time.

The linksRelays, and lasers

Orbiters relay the rovers' data today: a rover talks to an orbiter passing overhead, which sends it on to the big dishes of NASA's Deep Space Network on Earth.

Lasers can carry far more than radio. In 2023 NASA's Deep Space Optical Communications experiment on the Psyche spacecraft sent high-definition video to Earth by laser from 31 million km away.
