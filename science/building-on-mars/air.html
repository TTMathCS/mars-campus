Air

Oxygen from carbon dioxide and from water, diluted with the nitrogen and argon of the Mars air. The first oxygen ever made on another planet was made on Mars in 2021.

What a person needsAbout 0.8 kg of oxygen a day

A person uses about 0.8 kg of oxygen a day and breathes out about 1 kg of carbon dioxide, which must be scrubbed out of the air. Oxygen is only part of breathable air: pure oxygen burns too easily, so it is mixed with an inert gas, as nitrogen fills 78% of Earth's air.

Making itOxygen from carbon dioxide and from water

From Mars air. MOXIE, a box the size of a car battery on the rover Perseverance, split carbon dioxide into oxygen and carbon monoxide at about 800 °C. From 2021 to 2023 it made 122 g of oxygen in 16 runs, up to 12 g an hour at 98% purity: the first oxygen made on another planet. Scaled up many times over, the same method could make the oxygen for people and for a rocket's trip home.

From water. Electrolysis splits water into hydrogen and oxygen: 1 kg of water gives 0.89 kg of oxygen. The hydrogen goes to rocket fuel (see rocket fuel).

The rest of the air. Nitrogen and argon, 4.5% of Mars air, are separated out and added. Plants help too: a lit garden turns carbon dioxide back into oxygen.
