How people can live on Mars with the science we have, topic by topic, each with how Jim's house does it. Sources are
listed on the live page; the few numbers that are our own estimates say so.

![The house in section](../../design/img/house-hero.jpg)

*Built the way Mars allows: dug into thick ice under 16 m of soil, a garden level under lamps, a plant that makes air
and water, reactors for power.*

| Topic | The science | In Jim's house |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Getting there** | Launch windows every 26 months; six to nine months each way. Landing heavy loads needs rockets; Starship is designed for 100 t loads but has not yet flown to Mars. Bring machines, make bulk materials on Mars. | Robots build first; Jim travels by the Wormhole Gate, ships and the pod are for the views |
| **Building** | Mars soil is crushed basalt with iron oxides and salts. Sinter it at about 1,100 °C, make sulfur concrete (about 50 MPa), or 3D-print. The air inside is the main load: 11 m of soil balances 70 kPa, 16 m a full atmosphere. | The Pentagon dug 70 m into frozen ground, sintered panels, 16 m of soil on top |
| **Water** | Ice at the poles and under the middle latitudes. Bake it out of soil or melt a well deep in clean ice; about 130 kWh a tonne. Remove the perchlorate. The Space Station recycles 98% of its water. | 1.5 million t of ice from the dig; the L2 lake is the reserve |
| **Air** | A person uses about 0.8 kg of oxygen a day. MOXIE made oxygen from Mars air (122 g, up to 12 g an hour, 98% pure). Water electrolysis: 1 kg of water, 0.89 kg of oxygen. Nitrogen and argon from the air dilute it. | 70 kPa with 27% oxygen; the air plant on L4 |
| **Food** | Grown indoors under LEDs. About 40–50 m² of crops feed one person (NASA studies); our estimate of the light: about 100 kWh a day per person. | L2's orchards, farm, lake and meadow; two years of food in store |
| **Energy** | 43% of Earth's sunlight, none at night, weeks of dark in a global dust storm; wind is weak in thin air. Fission works in any weather. | Four 5 MWe reactors, 20 MWe, nothing on the ground |
| **Radiation** | 0.64 mSv a day on the ground, 1.8 in flight; about 1 Sv for a round trip with 500 days on Mars. Soil and water shield best. | Sleep under 16 m of soil: about 20 mSv a year |
| **Rocket fuel** | CO₂ + 2 H₂O → CH₄ + 2 O₂ (electrolysis and the Sabatier reaction). | 1,200 t per ship from 585 t of water and 715 t of CO₂ |
| **Talking to Earth** | 3 to 22 minutes each way; two weeks without contact every 26 months; lasers carry far more than radio. | Relays and a laser link (chapter 09) |
| **Every factor** | A table of 17 conditions and the answer to each: thin air, unbreathable air, cold, daily swings, wind, dust, dust storms, weak sunlight, radiation, ultraviolet, toxic soil, marsquakes, meteorites, frozen ground, low gravity, distance, keeping Mars clean. | 16 m of soil, insulated walls in the ice, four reactors, two years of food; slots in the Crown |
| **Health** | Bone loss of 1–1.5% a month in weightlessness; 0.38 g's long-term effect unknown; no evacuation; isolation tested by Mars-500 (520 days) and NASA's CHAPEA (378 days). | Gym, 50 m pool and baths on L1, gardens on L2, the medical centre on L3 |
| **Protecting Mars** | The Outer Space Treaty (1967) and COSPAR's rules: cleaned spacecraft, no special regions; rules for crewed missions being drafted. | A sealed house that lets nothing living out |
