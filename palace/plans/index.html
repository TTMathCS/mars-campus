The Crown and the Pentagon

Jim's retirement house on Arcadia Planitia, Mars, and the spaceport 30 km east where the ships from Earth land. These are the floor plans. Nothing is built in 3D yet.

On a phone, swipe sideways across a drawing to see all of it.

Above the ground: the Crown. A solid white ring 280 m across, held 40 m above the plain on five legs. Its five points rise to 66 m. It has no glass walls: narrow window slots look out to the horizon, and shutters close them in a storm.

Below the ground: the Pentagon. One solid five-sided block, 160 m on each side and five levels deep, with ten times the Crown's floor area. The five legs carry lifts straight down into its five corners, so the two halves work as one house.

Getting home. A pod lifts off at the spaceport, flies a 37 km scenic route in about four and a half minutes and lands on the Crown's east point. Later, more homes grow around the house in a spiral and become a city.

A-101

Site and flight corridor

Arcadia Planitia, about 44° N · north up

The house and the spaceport sit on the flat, ice-rich floor of Arcadia Planitia. The ice is the reason to live here: it gives water, air and rocket fuel.

The pod doesn't fly straight. Its 37 km scenic route crosses the Dune Sea with its dust devils, a 3 km crater and the Ice Cliffs, then circles the Crown before it lands. The numbers on the route are the shots of the flight video on sheet A-601.

The rover road is the backup for days when pods can't fly. In phase 2 a maglev tunnel runs 30 km straight between the two, 68 m underground.

From the top of the Crown (66 m) the horizon is 21 km away, so you can see the ships standing at the spaceport. Standing on the plain, the horizon is only 3.4 km away.

A-102

How the city grows from the house

10 km around the Crown · north up

Phase 1 · Port and home

The spaceport, the Crown and the Pentagon, the pod corridor and the rover road.

Phase 2 · First neighbours

13 homes, each a small crown over its own small pentagon, and the maglev tunnel to the spaceport.

Phase 3 · Arcadia City

Homes 14 to 233 and the civic buildings. The city reaches 3.8 km from the Crown.

Phase 4 · Connections

Tunnels and rail to other Mars cities.

Every new home takes the next seed of a sunflower spiral, turned 137.5° (the golden angle) from the one before. The spiral never needs replanning: it keeps growing outward and the homes stay evenly spaced, about 450 m apart.

Civic buildings take the Fibonacci seeds: 21 school, 34 hospital, 55 market hall, 89 university, 144 concert hall, 233 stadium. Those seeds line up on their own, so the civic buildings form one avenue running due north from the Crown.

Tunnels, the city's roots, link each home to its nearest older neighbour and through them back to the Pentagon. In a dust storm the whole city stays connected underground.

A-201

The Crown · main floor, 41 m above the plain

ring 240–280 m across · north up

The shell is sintered regolith, Mars soil fired into a white ceramic, with a titanium rim. The window slots are 1.2 m tall and set 3 m deep, so the sun never shines straight in, and outer shutters close them in a storm.

The ring is 20 m wide. Its five points, above the legs, rise to 66 m and hold a second floor (dashed). The Glide, a moving walkway, runs 767 m around the inside edge past every room.

The five legs stand on the Pentagon's corners and carry the lifts, water and power. Inside the ring, the Stone Garden holds about 340 sun-tracking mirrors that send daylight down the Sun Well into the Pentagon.

Storm mode: the shutters close, the Sun Well's iris closes, the pods stay in their hangar and daily life moves down to level L1.

A-301

The Pentagon · level plans

160 m sides · pick a level · north up
LevelFloorHeightAreaSectors 1 to 5

The Pentagon is one solid block of sintered regolith under 16 m of soil. That much soil weighs as much as the air inside pushes up: 101 kPa ÷ (1.7 t/m³ × 3.71 m/s²) ≈ 16 m. It also stops cosmic radiation.

Five rings, A on the inside to E on the outside, have 4 m streets between them. Five 5 m avenues run from the atrium out to the corner cores. No room is more than two minutes' walk from a lift.

Each corner core holds the lifts that come down one of the Crown's legs. The Stem, a glass lift in the middle of the atrium, stops at every level.

A-401

Section A–A · east to west through the centre

looking north · heights in metres above the plain

The cut passes through the Crown's east point (Arrival, with the pod deck on its roof) and its west dip (the Sunset lounge). The other three legs stand behind the cut.

The Crown's underside is at +40 m and its main floor at +41 m. The points rise to +66 m. The pod lands on the east point's roof and a lift takes it down into the hangar.

The Sun Well is the one big piece of glass in the house: a thick lens set into the ground under a solid iris. Below it, the atrium drops 68 m to the L5 floor.

A-501

Arcadia Spaceport · 30 km east of the house

phase 1 · north up

Three landing pads for ships from Earth stand 1.6 km from the terminal. A landing rocket throws rocks, so each pad has a berm and nothing else is near.

The fuel plant turns ground ice and the CO₂ in the air into methane and oxygen, so the ships refuel here for the trip back.

Arriving passengers ride a pressurised bus from the pad to the terminal, then walk through to the pod station on the west side, facing home.

Power comes from a solar field and a buried reactor. In phase 2 a maglev station opens under the terminal.

A-601

Pod flight home · the video

height above the plain against time

The pod. Four seats. It takes off and lands on rocket thrusters burning methane and oxygen, and cruises at 600 km/h on a lifting-body shape. Mars air has less than 1% of Earth's pressure, too thin for rotors to lift a cabin.

The flight. 37 km along the scenic route, 30 km in a straight line. About 4 min 30 s from lift-off to touchdown, late in the afternoon so you land as the blue Mars sunset starts. Highest point 400 m, lowest pass 30 m, under the ring.

Watching it. It plays in real time. You can switch between cockpit, chase and drone cameras, or skip at any moment.
