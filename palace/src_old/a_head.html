      Arcadia Palace

      A retirement palace designed for Mars. It sits on the western rim of a mesa above the plains of Arcadia Planitia, 44° north, facing a canyon where the sun sets. You arrive the way a resident would: a ship lands at Arcadia Spaceport, an air taxi flies you 1.4 km across the plain, and the gatehouse recognises you and opens the door. No key, no code: face, iris and the way you walk.

      Above ground is the palace from before: a glass Great Dome with the living room and garden, four wings under regolith, an observatory tower, garden domes, a hangar and a landing pad. Below it is the Deep, five levels cut into the rock and more than ten times the floor area, where life carries on whatever the weather: a salon level with a media lounge, a cinema, a jazz bar and a two-storey library; a grotto with a lagoon, a beach and a waterfall under a painted sky; a forest with a stream and a room where it rains; an engine level with a fusion plant, robots and an AI core; and a maglev station and two years of stores at the bottom.

      Every room answers to Mars. The air outside is less than one percent as thick as Earth's, it is colder than Antarctica, radiation reaches the ground, and gravity is 38 percent of what you are used to. Rock overhead stops radiation and dust storms, so the Deep is the safest place on the planet to grow old.

      How to look around

      • Journey (J): land at the spaceport, board the air taxi and fly to the palace. Esc skips it.
      • Tour: pick a place on the left, or use Next and Back. Drag to look around where you stand.
      • Walk: W A S D or the arrow keys, Shift to bound, Space to jump. When a prompt appears, press E: watch TV, sit, read a book, play the piano, call a lift, make it rain.
      • Cutaway: lifts the roofs and berms so you can see every surface room from above.
      • Plan: every level to scale, with tabs for the Deep. Click a room to go there.
      • Slide the clock to see the surface at sunrise, noon, a blue Mars sunset and night. The Deep keeps its own day.

      What's real and what's imagined

      The conditions are real Mars numbers. The site is imagined but plausible: Arcadia Planitia is low, flat and known for shallow ground ice. The house, its rooms and its machines are a design concept, not an engineering drawing: compact fusion, the air taxi and the biometric door are futures, not products.
