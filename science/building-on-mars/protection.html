Protecting Mars, and Earth

If there is, or ever was, life on Mars, people must not bury it under their own microbes before they find it; and nothing from Mars should harm Earth.

The rulesThe Outer Space Treaty and COSPAR

The Outer Space Treaty of 1967 asks nations, in its Article IX, to explore other worlds without contaminating them, and without bringing back anything that could harm Earth.

The international science committee COSPAR turns that into rules: spacecraft for Mars are cleaned and checked for microbes before launch, and only the cleanest may go to "special regions", where Earth microbes might grow because liquid water could occur, even briefly.

PeopleRules still being written

People cannot be sterilised: each of us carries trillions of microbes. So crewed missions need new rules, and COSPAR and NASA are drafting them: where people may go, how waste is kept in, and how to search for Martian life before Earth's spreads. Rock samples brought back to Earth are to be handled as hazardous until shown to be safe.

In Arcadia, Jim's home

Arcadia is sealed and recycles its air, water and waste inside, so nothing living is let out onto the plain.
