Food

Grown indoors, under lamps. On Mars, light for the crops is a bigger job than power for the home.

Why indoorsNot enough light, warmth or air outside

Mars gets 43% of Earth's sunlight, and dust cuts it further; plants also need warmth, air pressure and shelter from radiation. So Mars farms are greenhouses, or rooms underground lit by LEDs, which can give plants exactly the light they use.

Mars soil can grow plants once fixed. It has no organic matter and little usable nitrogen, and its perchlorate salts must be washed out first. In experiments, crops grew in copies of Mars soil with nutrients added.

Most growing is soilless: hydroponics, roots in flowing water with nutrients, gives the highest yields. NASA's crop studies found that about 40 to 50 m² of high-yield crops under strong light can feed one person.

Fish, insects and algae add protein; stores carry people through emergencies.

Crops under strong light take about 400 µmol of light a second on each square metre, 16 hours a day, and LEDs make about 3 µmol per joule, so 45 m² takes about 6 kW while the lamps are on: about 100 kWh a day for each person fed.

For scale

100 kWh a day is about three times the electricity an average Canadian home uses in a day. On Mars, power for food is a bigger job than power for the home: see energy.
