Health

Low gravity, medicine far from home, and isolation. Radiation is the other long-term risk: see shielding.

Low gravity0.38 g, and nobody knows what years of it do

In weightlessness the body unloads: astronauts lose about 1 to 1.5% of the bone in their hips and spine a month, and muscle too, unless they exercise hard, about two hours a day on the Space Station; fluids shift to the head, and some develop changes in their eyes. Mars' 0.38 g should help, but no one knows how much: the longest anyone has lived in partial gravity is about three days, on the Moon with Apollo 17.

MedicineNo evacuation

The next ship home may be two years away, and the trip takes months. A settlement needs its own doctors, surgery, medicines and scanners, and advice from Earth arrives 6 to 44 minutes after the question.

IsolationTested on Earth

Mars-500 (2010–2011) sealed six men in a mock spaceship in Moscow for 520 days, the length of a trip there and back; several of them slept poorly and grew inactive.

NASA's CHAPEA crew lived 378 days (2023–2024) in Mars Dune Alpha, a 3D-printed mock Mars home in Houston, with a real mission's delays in talking to Earth.
