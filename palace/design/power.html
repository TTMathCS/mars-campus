Chapter 05

Power

On Mars, power is life: it makes the air, melts the water, lights the farms and fuels the ships home. Sunlight is weak, a global dust storm can hide it for weeks, and Jim wants no panels spread over the ground, so Arcadia takes its power from the city's grid, as any home in a city does, and keeps enough of its own to ride through an outage. It has no power plant: no reactor to refuel or guard, no radiators, no panels on the ground.

What needs powerDemand in phase 1

The biggest single user is light: the farms and gardens on L2 grow under lamps. The second is the fuel plant at the spaceport, which turns ice and air into methane and oxygen for the ships and pods (chapter 07). Everything else, the air and water plants, the rooms, the foundry and the port, adds up to about a third.

The fuel plant is the one load that can wait. It runs hardest when the city has power to spare, at night and when the farms' lamps are off, and slows down when the city asks.

Future technology

The Crown's anti-gravity drives and the portals have no known physics, so their power needs are guesses. The design keeps 0.5 MW for them and has room to grow.

Where it comes fromFrom the city, and nothing on the ground

The city's grid. The city makes the power and brings it in; Arcadia buys it, as every home in the city does. It has no power plant of its own: no reactor to refuel, guard or one day take apart, no field of radiators to shed its heat. The power comes in on two buried cables by different routes (the grid, below), at the city feed on L4 (L4-01).

Its own, for an outage. Batteries of 20 MWh at Arcadia (L4-03) and 20 MWh at the port carry the evening peak and the first hours of any fault. Fuel cells (L4-20, and at the port) can burn the methane and oxygen kept for the ships: 1,000 tonnes of propellant keeps 8 MW going for about 9 days.

No solar field. Solar power works on Mars (Spirit, Opportunity and InSight ran on it), but a field big enough for this home would cover the plain in panels, and a global dust storm can hide the sun for weeks; one ended Opportunity in 2018.

Real technology

Buried direct-current cables already join countries on Earth: NordLink carries 1,400 MW over 623 km between Norway and Germany (2021). Battery plants of hundreds of megawatt-hours steady grids today, and solid-oxide fuel cells run on methane.

The gridTwo cables, two routes

The city's power comes in on two buried direct-current cables by different routes, so a cut in one leaves the other: one runs 30 km beside the rover road from the spaceport, where the city's grid reaches, and the other comes through the first of the city's tunnels (L5-16). Each, at ±20 kV, can carry 15 MW, the house and the port together with room to spare; at full load about 1.5% is lost on the way, less in the cold ground.

An optical fibre runs in each trench for data, as the backup to the radio links (chapter 09).

Inside Arcadia, power is distributed as direct current too, because the batteries and most machines work on DC. The switchgear is on L4 (L4-04), beside the batteries.

HeatWarmth from heat pumps, kept in by the ground

With no reactor there is no spare heat, so Arcadia makes its own warmth. Heat pumps on L4 (L4-02) take the heat back out of the air leaving the rooms and out of the machines beside them (the air and water plants, the house mind's computers) and put it into warm water for the floors, the ice melt and the gardens: about 2 MW of warmth in the coldest weeks for about 0.7 MW of electricity.

A tank of hot water (L4-03) covers the evening peak. The thick insulation of the Pentagon and the frozen ground round it keep most of the warmth in (chapter 08), and the Crown's rooms are kept warm the same way.
