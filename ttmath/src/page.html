TTMath Mars Campus

An imagined TTMath campus on Mars, set at "Dingo Gap" in Gale Crater. The ground around your starting point, about 260 × 260 m, is real: NASA JPL's 3D model built from Curiosity rover photos (around sol 528) and HiRISE orbital images. The campus, the landscape beyond, the rovers and the sky effects are simulated.

The campus is one building around a paved courtyard. Two long curved wings sweep out from the Math Palace, their glass fronts facing each other under a deep canopy, and a gateway with the campus name closes the courtyard. Glass links join each wing to the dome.

The classroom wing has a math classroom with desks and a whiteboard, a lobby, a coding lab with code on every monitor, and a seminar room. The other wing has the Café π with its espresso bar, the reception with the front desk, a library and a reading room.

The Math Palace is a glass dome 56 m across whose lattice follows the spirals of a sunflower, 21 ribs one way and 34 the other. Inside, a balcony rings a rotunda sunk 10 m into the ground, clad in Siena, Carrara and black Marquina marble. Two grand stairs sweep down to a Penrose floor inlaid with brass, a sunflower of 1,600 brass seeds, twelve math exhibits, the five Platonic solids, a golden Möbius strip and a 24 m Foucault pendulum.

Materials are shaded like the real thing: veined and polished marble, brass, oak and walnut, fabric, painted plaster, glass that reflects the actual surroundings, and Mars dust settled on the outside. Light from every fitting is traced for soft shadows and dark corners a few seconds after the page opens.

Things to try

  • Walk up the ridge ahead: after a few steps the campus comes into view. Go through the gateway into the courtyard (hold Shift to run); the glass doors of the lobby and the reception open as you come near.
  • Visit the classrooms: solve the quadratic on the whiteboard, read the code on the lab monitors, check the day's timetable on the lobby screen and the menu in the café.
  • Inside, take the grand stairs down and read the exhibits. Count the spirals in the floor's sunflower and in the dome: they are Fibonacci numbers.
  • The light studs along the avenue sit 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 and 34 m from the door.
  • Time the pendulum: one swing takes 16 s in Mars gravity, against 9.8 s on Earth.
  • Press play and watch the sunset turn to twilight: the windows light up the courtyard, clouds keep shining after the Sun is gone, and stars come out.
  • In twilight, find Earth and the Moon low in the west, and Phobos rising.
  • Jump: in Mars gravity you stay in the air about three times as long.
  • Turn on Sound for the wind, your footsteps and the echo under the dome.
