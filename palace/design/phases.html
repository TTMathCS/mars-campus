Chapter 10

Building it

Robots first. They land in early 2023, two launch windows before Jim, and work for four years: power and fuel at the spaceport, the road and the cable, the Pentagon dug 70 m into the ice and built from its own soil, the Crown assembled and lifted to its height. When Jim lands in June 2027, Arcadia is warm and full of air, and its gardens are planted. Then the city grows round it, one seed of the sunflower at a time.

The order of workPower first, people last

Each step makes what the next one needs: power for the machines, then water and fuel, then a road to carry them, then a hole in the ice to build in. Nobody lives on Mars until it is all running.

  1. Land and switch on, 2023. The first 12 cargo ships land on the open plain of landing site AP-1 in early 2023. Robots unload them and bury the spaceport's two reactors in their vault; from mid-2023 everything runs on their 10 MW (chapter 05).
  2. Water and fuel, 2023 to 2025. The ice mine opens north of the terminal; the fuel plant follows, line by line, and from 2025 makes the methane and oxygen for every ship that flies home (chapter 07). The robots build the three pads.
  3. The corridor, 2023 to 2024. The rover road and the trench to Arcadia's site, 30 km west, with the DC cable and the optical fibre in it (chapters 05 and 06).
  4. The dig, 2024 to 2025. 3.1 million m³ out of an open pit 70 m deep, about 4,300 m³ a sol. Below 14 m most of it is ice: 1.5 million t go to the ice store and the fuel plant. Kilns at the pit's edge fire the first panels from the soil (chapter 03).
  1. The Pentagon, from the bottom up, 2025 to 2026. L5 and L4 first, with Arcadia's two reactors (L4-01) and the air and water plants; then L3, where the kilns move indoors (L3-07), L2 and L1. The roof closes late in 2026 and 16 m of the dug soil goes back on top.
  2. Air and warmth, 2026 to 2027. L4 makes the air through 2026: 560 t of oxygen, and 1,560 t of nitrogen and argon, left when the carbon dioxide is frozen out of about 52,000 t of Mars air (chapter 08). Early in 2027 it fills the Pentagon, level by level, and the reactors' heat warms it.
  3. The Crown, 2026 to 2027. Five pads, then the ring, assembled 2 m above them one 36° part at a time: shell panels from the kilns, 90,000 t of ice cut from the dig. Closed and pressure-tested, it lifts to +40 m in April 2027; the Orb rises from its dock to the middle; the Stone Garden is laid underneath (chapter 02).
  4. The gardens, 2025 to 2027. Saplings and seeds come with the second wave and grow in a ship's hold at the port. Once L2 holds air, early in 2027, they are planted in soil made from washed Mars soil and compost, and the lake is filled.
  5. Jim lands, June 2027, on pad 1, with the third wave. A health check at the terminal, then the pod home (chapter 06). He makes the journey by ship this once: when he leaves Earth, the Orb, with the Wormhole Gate in it, is still being built.

Robots firstMachines that work on their own

Earth is 3 to 22 minutes away by radio (chapter 09), too far to drive a digger from. So the robots work on their own: the house mind (L3-14) gives them the day's work, such as "dig this block to 70 m" or "set these panels", and they plan the moves, avoid each other and stop when something looks wrong. People on Earth check the work once a day, as engineers check mines on Earth from far away.

They run on electricity from the grid, charged in the rover hall (L5-19), and live, once the Pentagon is built, in the rover hall and garage (L5-17, L5-18) and the robot bay (L1-12). The robot foundry (L3-06) prints their spare parts, the test halls (L3-12) try every machine before use, and the training rooms (L3-22) teach new tasks. From 2029 two tunnel boring machines (L5-14) dig the maglev tunnel and the city's roots.

Real technology

Driverless haul trucks and drills already work iron-ore mines in Western Australia, run from a control room 1,500 km away. NASA has built and tested robot diggers for the Moon and Mars, such as RASSOR, and teams in NASA's 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge (2015–2019) printed habitats from materials that could be made on Mars.

From Earth, and from MarsMachines from Earth, everything else from Mars

Three waves of ships. What can't be made on Mars yet comes from Earth: the reactors, the robots and their kilns, the machines of the fuel plant and of life support, the wiring, lamps and electronics, the seeds and saplings, the bees and fish, Jim's own things, and the drives and portals of future technology. About 3,600 t in all, in 36 cargo ships of about 100 t each, the load a ship like SpaceX's Starship is designed to land (our estimate).

The cargo ships stay. The first two waves land on the open plain east of the pads, a kilometre apart, and are not flown home: their holds become stores, one of them the first greenhouse, and their steel later goes to the foundry. From 2027 the ships land on the pads, refuel and fly back, as in chapter 07.

Mars gives the rest: the Pentagon's panels, about 600,000 t fired from its own soil; the Crown's shell and ice, 129,000 t; the water in the lake and the tanks, about 28,000 t; the 2,100 t of air; the gardens' soil. Over 200 t made on Mars for every tonne shipped.

After JimThe city grows, seed by seed

Arcadia is the first home of Arcadia City. The others take the seeds of the sunflower spiral round it (chapter 01), each a small crown over a small pentagon, built by the same robots from the same soil and ice.

Phase 2, 2029 to 2038: the first neighbours. Thirteen homes within 1 km, about a hundred people, the food for whom already grows on L2 (chapter 08). In 2029 two tunnel boring machines start the maglev, 68 m down, one from L5 and one from the spaceport; they meet in the middle, and it opens in 2033 (chapter 06). Power grows to 12 MW within the four reactors (chapter 05).

Phase 3, 2038 to the early 2060s: Arcadia City. About twenty homes a window, 233 in all, out to 3.8 km, and about 2,000 people. The civic avenue fills in as the city reaches its seeds: the school about 2040, the hospital 2042, the market hall 2044, the university 2049, the concert hall 2053 and the stadium, the 233rd seed, in the early 2060s. A larger power plant takes L4's fusion-ready bay (L4-02).

Phase 4, from the 2060s: other cities. Rail and tunnels to cities near by, portals to those far away, sharing power the way countries on Earth do.

If it runs lateEvery step has a way round

  • A late ship or a late step. The Crown lifts in April 2027, two months before Jim lands, and every wave brings spares. If a whole wave is lost, Jim's launch moves to the next window, in January 2029.
  • Dust storms slow the outdoor work but don't stop it: the reactors don't need the sun, and the robots find their way by radar.
  • Something breaks. The foundry prints most parts; the rest come as spares with each wave; nothing depends on one machine.
  • The future technology isn't ready: the Crown stands on its five pads (chapter 02), stairs and robot carriers do the portals' work (chapter 06), and the ships do the Gate's.
