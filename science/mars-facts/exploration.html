Exploration

Everything on these pages was measured by spacecraft. These are the missions that did it: flybys, orbiters, landers, rovers and a helicopter, from five nations and Europe, over sixty years. Many attempts failed; those that worked are the reason we know Mars.

  1. 1965 · Mariner 4NASA.

    The first close-up pictures of another planet, 21 of them, from a flyby on 14 and 15 July 1965: a cratered surface, and air far thinner than expected.

  2. 1971 · Mariner 9NASA.

    The first spacecraft to orbit another planet. It arrived during a global dust storm, waited for the dust to settle, then mapped the planet: the giant volcanoes, the canyons named Valles Marineris after it, and dry riverbeds.

  3. 1971 · Mars 2 and Mars 3Soviet Union.

    Mars 2's lander crashed, the first human-made object on Mars. Mars 3 made the first soft landing and sent signals for about 20 seconds before it fell silent.

  4. 1976 · Viking 1 and Viking 2NASA.

    Two orbiters and two landers, the first to work for years on the surface: the first weather records from another planet, colour pictures of a rocky red desert under a pink sky, and experiments that tested the soil for life and found no convincing sign of it.

  5. 1997 · Mars Pathfinder and Mars Global SurveyorNASA.

    Pathfinder bounced down on airbags and let out Sojourner, the first rover on another planet, 10.6 kg on six wheels. Global Surveyor mapped the heights of the whole planet by laser, and found the magnetised crust of the south.

  6. 2001 · Mars OdysseyNASA.

    Found hydrogen, the sign of ice, just under the surface at high latitudes, and relayed the rovers' data home; it worked at Mars for more than two decades.

  7. 2003 · Mars ExpressEuropean Space Agency.

    Europe's first Mars mission. Its radar measured the ice of the south polar cap. Its lander, Beagle 2, fell silent on landing and was found on the surface in 2015, partly unfolded.

  8. 2004 · Spirit and OpportunityNASA.

    Two rovers planned for 90 sols. Spirit drove until 2010, Opportunity until the dust storm of 2018, 45 km in all. They found rocks that had formed in water.

  9. 2006 · Mars Reconnaissance OrbiterNASA.

    Its HiRISE camera sees things the size of a table from orbit: new craters, avalanches, dust devils. Its radar maps the buried ice of the northern plains.

  10. 2008 · PhoenixNASA.

    Landed at 68° N, scraped away the soil and touched ice, found perchlorate salts, and saw snow falling from the clouds.

  11. 2012 · CuriosityNASA.

    A rover of 900 kg with a nuclear power source, lowered into Gale crater by a rocket-powered crane. It found an ancient lake once fit for life, organic molecules in old rock, and measured the radiation on the ground. The TTMath campus stands on its ground.

  12. 2014 · MAVEN and MangalyaanNASA; Indian Space Research Organisation.

    MAVEN measures how Mars loses its air to space. India's Mars Orbiter Mission was the first Asian mission to reach Mars orbit, at its first attempt.

  13. 2016 · ExoMars Trace Gas OrbiterEuropean Space Agency and Roscosmos.

    Measures the rare gases of the air, such as methane. Its test lander, Schiaparelli, crashed.

  14. 2018 · InSightNASA.

    A seismometer on the ground recorded 1,319 marsquakes until 2022, and mapped the crust, the mantle and the liquid core.

  15. 2021 · Hope, Tianwen-1 and PerseveranceUnited Arab Emirates; China; NASA.

    Hope studies the weather of the whole planet from a high orbit. Tianwen-1 put the Zhurong rover down in Utopia Planitia, making China the second nation to drive on Mars. Perseverance landed in Jezero crater, collects rock samples, made oxygen from the air with MOXIE, and carried Ingenuity, which made the first powered flight on another planet and flew 72 times.

More are planned, among them the European Space Agency's Rosalind Franklin rover, built to drill 2 m into the ground to look for signs of life.
