The Crown and the Pentagon

Jim's retirement house on Arcadia Planitia, Mars, and the spaceport 30 km east where the ships from Earth land. Rev B is drawn from your answers to Rev A. Nothing is built in 3D yet.

On a phone, swipe sideways across a drawing to see all of it.

Above the ground: the Crown. A solid white ring 276 m across floats 40 m above the plain on anti-gravity drives hidden in its five spires. Nothing touches the ground. The spires rise to 90 m. At the centre hovers the Orb, a mirror sphere 40 m across with the Wormhole Gate inside. There are no glass walls, only narrow window slots with storm shutters.

Below the ground: the Pentagon. One solid five-sided block, 160 m on each side and five levels deep, with 10.4 times the Crown's floor area. There are no lifts anywhere. Portals in the spires, in the Orb and on every level carry you across in one step.

Getting home. A pod lifts off at the spaceport and flies a 37 km scenic route. Near the end it dives into a dust storm, breaks out into the sunset and flies straight into the hangar in the Crown's east spire. Later, more homes grow around the house in a spiral and become a city.

A-001

The Crown · bird's-eye view from the south

axonometric · true scale · looking north

Nothing touches the ground. Anti-gravity drives in the five spires hold the ring 40 m above the plain; the rings drawn on the ground show where their fields reach. The spires rise to 90 m and the dips between them to 50 m.

The Orb floats on its own over the Sun Well, from 52 m to 92 m up. Its mirror skin is also the Sun Well's top mirror: the garden mirrors aim sunlight at its underside and it bounces the light down into the Pentagon.

The dashed lines are the window slots, one row along the main floor and a second row in each spire. Everything else is solid white shell. The small dome at the front right is the TTMath campus dome from demo 1, drawn at the same scale.

A-101

Site and flight corridor

Arcadia Planitia, about 44° N · north up

The house and the spaceport sit on the flat, ice-rich floor of Arcadia Planitia. The ice is the reason to live here: it gives water, air and rocket fuel.

The pod doesn't fly straight. Its 37 km scenic route crosses the Dune Sea with its dust devils, a 3 km crater, the Ice Cliffs and a dust storm, then circles the Crown before it flies into the hangar. The numbers on the route are the shots of the flight video on sheet A-601.

The rover road is the backup for days when pods can't fly. In phase 2 a maglev tunnel for cargo runs 30 km straight between the two, 68 m underground.

From the top of the Crown (90 m) the horizon is 25 km away, so you can see the ships standing at the spaceport. Standing on the plain, the horizon is only 3.4 km away.

A-102

How the city grows from the house

10 km around the Crown · north up

Phase 1 · Port and home

The spaceport, the Crown and the Pentagon, the pod corridor and the rover road.

Phase 2 · First neighbours

13 homes, each a small floating crown over its own small pentagon, and the maglev tunnel for cargo to the spaceport.

Phase 3 · Arcadia City

Homes 14 to 233 and the civic buildings. The city reaches 3.8 km from the Crown.

Phase 4 · Connections

Portals, tunnels and rail to other Mars cities.

Every new home takes the next seed of a sunflower spiral, turned 137.5° (the golden angle) from the one before. The spiral never needs replanning: it keeps growing outward and the homes stay evenly spaced, about 450 m apart.

Civic buildings take the Fibonacci seeds: 21 school, 34 hospital, 55 market hall, 89 university, 144 concert hall, 233 stadium. Those seeds line up on their own, so the civic buildings form one avenue running due north from the Crown.

People move between homes by portal. The tunnels, the city's roots, carry cargo, water and power from each home to its nearest older neighbour and back to the Pentagon, so in a dust storm the whole city stays supplied.

A-201

The Crown · main floor, 41 m above the plain

ring 244–276 m across · north up

The shell is sintered regolith, Mars soil fired into a white ceramic, with a titanium rim. The window slots are 1.2 m tall and set 3 m deep, so the sun never shines straight in, and outer shutters close them in a storm.

The ring is 16 m wide. Each of the five spires holds a second floor (dashed), an anti-gravity drive in the tip above it and a portal. The Glide, a moving walkway, runs 779 m around the inside edge past every room.

There are no legs and no lifts. The portals link the five spires with each other, with the Orb and with every level of the Pentagon. The Orb floats above the Sun Well, and its three floors are listed with the rooms below.

Storm mode: the shutters close, the Sun Well's iris closes, the pods stay in their hangar and daily life moves down to level L1.

A-301

The Pentagon · level plans

160 m sides · pick a level · north up
LevelFloorHeightAreaSectors 1 to 5

The Pentagon is one solid block of sintered regolith under 16 m of soil. That much soil weighs as much as the air inside pushes up: 101 kPa ÷ (1.7 t/m³ × 3.71 m/s²) ≈ 16 m. It also stops cosmic radiation.

Five rings, A on the inside to E on the outside, have 4 m streets between them. Five 5 m avenues run from the atrium out to the corner cores. No room is more than two minutes' walk from a portal.

There are no lifts. Each corner core holds a portal to one of the Crown's spires, plus a stair between the levels. The portal column in the middle of the atrium has a portal on every level, reached by five bridges. The second master suite is on L1, sector 1.

A-401

Section A–A · east to west through the centre

looking north · heights in metres above the plain

The cut passes through the Crown's east spire (Arrival, with the pod hangar), the Orb at the centre and the west dip (the Sunset lounge).

The Crown floats: its underside is at +40 m and nothing below it touches the ground. The main floor is at +41 m and the spires reach +90 m. The pod flies into the hangar through a door facing the garden.

The Orb hovers from +52 m to +92 m, with floors at +64, +72 and +80 and the Wormhole Gate in its middle. The Sun Well below it is the one big piece of glass in the house, a thick lens under a solid iris. The atrium drops 68 m to the L5 floor.

A-501

Arcadia Spaceport · 30 km east of the house

phase 1 · north up

Three landing pads for ships from Earth stand 1.6 km from the terminal. A landing rocket throws rocks, so each pad has a berm and nothing else is near.

The fuel plant turns ground ice and the CO₂ in the air into methane and oxygen, so the ships refuel here for the trip back.

Arriving passengers ride a pressurised bus from the pad to the terminal, then walk through to the pod station on the west side, facing home.

Power comes from a solar field and a buried reactor. In phase 2 a maglev station for cargo opens under the terminal.

A-601

Pod flight home · the video

height above the plain against time

The pod. Four seats. It takes off and lands on rocket thrusters burning methane and oxygen, and cruises at 600 km/h on a lifting-body shape. Mars air has less than 1% of Earth's pressure, too thin for rotors to lift a cabin.

The flight. 37 km along the scenic route, 30 km in a straight line, about 4 min 40 s from lift-off to the hangar. It leaves late in the afternoon, flies into a dust storm after the Ice Cliffs and breaks out into the blue Mars sunset with the Crown ahead. Highest point 400 m, lowest pass 30 m, under the ring.

Watching it. It plays in real time. You can switch between cockpit, chase and drone cameras, or skip at any moment.
